The jargon, explained

Okay, but what's a "forward deployed engineer"?

Imagine you hired a really good engineer, but instead of sitting somewhere else writing a report about your business, they pulled up a chair next to your team.

They watch how the work actually happens. They notice that someone in finance spends every Thursday afternoon copying things from email into the ERP, and that support answers the same eleven questions by hand. Then they build the thing that does that, plug it into your systems, and make sure it doesn't break at three in the morning.

That's it. That's the job. We do it for AI specifically, because right now that's where the gap between "cool demo" and "actually running" is widest, and we happen to like closing that gap.

You get working software in weeks, not a roadmap. And when we leave, it's yours: the code, the docs, and a team that knows how it works.

What we do

Four things, done properly.

Most engagements are some mix of these. We'll tell you up front which ones you actually need, and which ones you don't.

week 0–1

Mapping

Where does AI actually pay off here? We spend the first days shadowing your team, reading your data and being politely nosy. Then we come back with a short list: the workflows worth automating, the ones that aren't, and what each one would take.

the main event

Build & integrate

Agents, retrieval over your documents, copilots for your team, automations between CRM, ERP, mail and docs. Built on your real data, and built to be trusted:

  • evals from day one, so we know when it gets worse
  • permissions that respect who may see what
  • observability, so you know what it's doing
  • a human in the loop wherever the stakes say so
coming along

Private / on-prem inference

Some data shouldn't leave the building. We can run models on hardware you control, in Sweden, in your rack or ours, so nothing sensitive touches a third-party API. This one's newer for us, so we'll be honest about what's ready and what's still cooking.

handover, not lock-in

Enablement

We don't want to be your AI team forever. Documented code, a handover that actually hands things over, and training so your own engineers can keep it running and build the next thing themselves. Then we stay on call in case something gets weird.

How it goes

Eight weeks, give or take.

Every company is different, but the shape of an engagement is pretty consistent. Here's the arc.

1
Week 0

Embed

We show up, get accounts, sit with your team and ask a lot of slightly annoying questions. We read your data before we form opinions about it.

2
Week 1–2

Prototype on real data

Not a slide. A rough thing, running on your actual documents and systems. You poke at it, it breaks in interesting ways, we learn what matters.

3
Week 3–8

Harden

Evals, permissions, monitoring, guardrails. The unglamorous part that turns a demo into something you'd let near a customer.

4
After

Hand over, stay on call

Your team owns it: code, docs, dashboards, the lot. We're one message away if something gets weird, and we'll say so if it's time for round two.

Honesty policy

Things we will actually say out loud.

We're engineers, and we'd like to still be friends with you in a year. So we say the awkward thing early.

"AI is the wrong tool for this. Here's a cheaper fix."
"That demo will fall apart on your real data. Let's test first."
"We don't know yet. Give us three days."
"That data can't go to that API. Here's what we do instead."
"You can run this without us now."
"Honestly? A spreadsheet would do."
